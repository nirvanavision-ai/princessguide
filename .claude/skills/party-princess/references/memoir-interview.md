# The Memoir Interview

This is what separates the book from a listicle. Advice is free; the reader
buys the book for the night the heel snapped in the Wynn valet line.

## How to run it

### One question at a time. Open. No options.

This overrides the original design. The author's own instruction, 2026-08-13:

> *"Ask me one at a time, because I feel like you're giving advice… I can choose
> the one you like… Just ask me and give me a chance to respond."*

> *"It didn't have to answer all of them."* · *"Ah, too much."*

Two separate failures were happening and both are fixable:

**Batching overwhelms.** A block of six prompts gets skimmed and half-answered.
She named this directly.

**Multiple-choice options contaminate the answer.** This is the subtler and more
damaging one. When you offer four options, you've shown her what you're looking
for — *"I can choose the one you like."* She then answers toward your options
instead of from her own memory, and the whole point of this interview is to get
material you could not have produced. **Your options are, definitionally, things
you already thought of.** They cannot be her voice.

So:

- **Ask one open question. Stop. Wait.** Then ask the next.
- **Do not offer options.** No `AskUserQuestion` menus for memoir or voice
  material. Plain questions in plain text.
- **Silence is an answer.** If she skips it, leave the placeholder and move on
  without re-asking.
- **Follow up on the specific, not the general.** If an answer is thin, ask for
  one concrete detail — the venue, the shoe, the price, the exact thing said.
  One follow-up. Then write.

<sub>The question banks below are kept as **your** memory aids for what to ask
next. Do not read the trigger lists aloud to her — they exist so you know the
territory, not so she can pick from them.</sub>

### Recording

- Save answers **verbatim** to `book/.memoir/chapter-N.md` under a `## Q:` /
  `## A:` structure, with the date. Never paraphrase into the notebook.
- Keep transcription errors and garbled words intact, marked `(?)` where you've
  guessed. Ask her to correct them; never silently fix a name.
- If the author gives you three sentences, that's a full anecdote — expand it
  into 150–250 words of scene using *only* the facts given, plus generic
  atmosphere. Do not invent a second character, a location, a quote, or an
  outcome she didn't supply.

### Voice notes are the best channel

Both of the richest sessions so far were her talking, not typing. Spoken answers
are unguarded, they ramble into material nobody would type, and the rambling is
where the book is. When she offers to record, always take it.

## Rendering an anecdote

Set the scene in one line. Land the specific detail they gave you, unchanged.
Deliver the turn. Extract the rule — because in this book every story is
secretly a policy document.

Mark it in the file so it's findable:

```markdown
### From the Author's Own Files
```

## Question banks

### Chapter 1 — The Glow-Up

1. **The door.** "Tell me about the worst door you've ever been turned away
   from — or made to wait at long enough that it counted."
   Triggers: *LA, and the list had my name spelled wrong* / *Vegas, and the
   group was the problem* / *Miami, and I was underdressed by local standards* /
   *I've never been turned away and I'd like that on the record* / *Skip.*
2. **The shoe.** "Which shoe betrayed you, and where were you standing when
   it happened?"
   Triggers: *A stiletto on a cobblestone/grate* / *A brand-new pair, worn out
   of the box* / *A platform that was fine until hour six* / *Skip.*
3. **Prep.** "What's the one prep step you'd never skip, even at T-minus 90
   with the car downstairs?"

### Chapter 2 — Home Base & The Purse

1. **The save.** "What did you once pull out of a tiny bag that rescued an
   entire night?"
   Triggers: *A safety pin* / *A phone battery, for someone else* / *Blister
   patches* / *Cash, when a card wouldn't run* / *Skip.*
2. **The retirement.** "What did you used to carry that you've since banned
   from the bag, and what happened?"
3. **The return.** "Describe your apartment at 4 a.m. after a good night — is
   it staged for you, or is it a crime scene?"

### Chapter 3 — Transport

1. **The disaster.** "What's the transport failure that made you change how you
   do this permanently?"
   Triggers: *Surge pricing at 2 a.m.* / *A driver who couldn't find the door* /
   *A cancelled ride in the cold* / *Someone got left behind* / *Skip.*
2. **The handler.** "Has a sober friend ever run your night — and did they save
   it or ruin it?"
3. **The car.** "What actually lives in your car for a night out?"

### Chapter 4 — The VIP Playbook

1. **The table.** "Tell me about the best table night you've had — or the one
   where the check arrived and the room went quiet."
   Triggers: *A host who took care of us all night* / *A minimum we badly
   misjudged* / *A bottle sign that killed* / *Someone else's guests, our tab* /
   *Skip.*
2. **The romance variable.** "What's the most memorable partner-at-the-table
   situation you've navigated?"
   Triggers: *A boyfriend who turned a girls' night into a supervision detail* /
   *A friend's partner who made the check a competition* / *A man who needed to
   talk to the DJ* / *It went well, genuinely* / *Skip.*
3. **The host.** "Who's the best VIP host you've ever worked with, and what did
   they do that others don't?"

### Chapter 5 — Royalty Rules

1. **The staff member.** "Which bartender, server, doorman, or valet do you still
   think about — and what did they do?"
2. **The boundary.** "Tell me about a No you held. What did you say, and what
   did they do about it?"
   Triggers: *A no that got repeated verbatim four times* / *A handoff to
   security* / *A friend I had to handle* / *Skip.*
3. **The grace clause.** "When were *you* the rude one, and how did you fix it?"

### Chapter 6 — Emergencies & Don'ts

1. **The emergency.** "What went catastrophically wrong on a night out — and how
   did you handle it?"
   Triggers: *A broken heel* / *A drink down the front of a rented dress* / *A
   friend who vanished* / *A dead phone at the worst possible hour* / *Skip.*
2. **The Don't.** "Which rule on the Don'ts list did you learn the hard way?"
3. **The recovery.** "What's the fastest bathroom repair job you've ever pulled
   off, and what was in the bag?"

### Chapter 7 — After-Party & Recovery

1. **The after-party.** "Describe the greatest after-party you've ever hosted.
   What made it work?"
   Triggers: *The food arrived at exactly the right moment* / *The music never
   broke* / *The right eleven people and not a twelfth* / *It ended cleanly,
   which is the rarest one* / *Skip.*
2. **The Sonos incident.** "Has your audio setup ever failed you in front of a
   room full of people?"
3. **The morning.** "What's your genuine, non-aspirational morning-after ritual?"

### Chapter 8 — Quick Travel Tips

1. **The trip that went wrong.** "What happened on a night out far from home
   that you'd never have had happen in your own city?"
   Triggers: *A bag that didn't arrive* / *A card that stopped working abroad* /
   *A dress code nobody warned me about* / *A hotel too far from everything* /
   *Skip.*
2. **The one-night city.** "Where would you fly for a single night, and what
   would you do the second you landed?"
3. **The packing rule.** "What's the one thing you always carry on, and what did
   it cost you to learn that?"

## The notebook format

`book/.memoir/chapter-N.md`:

```markdown
# Memoir Notes — Chapter N: <Title>
> Raw author material. Quoted, never rewritten. Do not delete.

**Session:** <date>

## Q: <question as asked>
**Selected:** <option, if any>

## A:
<the author's words, verbatim>

**Follow-up:** <question>
## A:
<verbatim>

---
**Used in:** <section name, once drafted>
```
