## Why this is the centerpiece of the chapter

**The offense was not the eye.** Read the order she put it in: poked her eye,
*didn't apologize*, **tried to take attention off Kanye onto himself.** The
escalation lands on the third item. The eye was an accident. The refusal to
apologize was rude. Making himself the event was the disqualifying one.

That's the entire value system of the book, stated by its violation. She went
for the music. He turned the night into a referendum on himself. It's the exact
inverse of her own rule from voice note 002 — *"I don't need the attention, I
don't want to talk about me, just fucking vibe, dude."* She dumped a man for
being extra.

Chapter 4's Romance Variable currently describes "ego-driven drama at the table"
in the abstract. This replaces the abstraction. **The rule the chapter should
extract:** a partner who cannot let the night be about the night is not a
scheduling problem, he's a structural one. You are the general manager of the
table. GMs make staffing decisions.

## The line

> **"He called it 'emotional execution'. I called it a Sunday night."**

The best sentence she has given me and it should not be touched. His phrase is
enormous, self-serious, and about his own interior weather. Hers is four words
and a shrug. The comedy is entirely in the size difference — she doesn't argue
with the melodrama, she just declines to match its scale. Deadpan working
perfectly.

**Placement:** strong candidate for the Ch. 4 epigraph, though it may be too good
to spend there — it could equally close the Romance Variable section as the
verdict. Her call. Banked in `voice/quotes.md`.

<sub>One caution worth stating once: this is a real, recent, identifiable
ex-partner. Memoir can absolutely include him, but the first name should
probably change in print, and the material should stay to what he did on that
night rather than a general character assessment. She may also simply not want a
break-up in the book at all. Her call entirely — flagging, not advising.</sub>
