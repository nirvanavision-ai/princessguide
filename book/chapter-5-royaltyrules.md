## Scarlett's

Miami. I have brought a friend to Scarlett's for the first time and she is
having one of the great nights of her life. So are the girls working. Both of
those are my job.

They were always going to earn. Easily and happily means it did not cost them
the night. Nobody at my table gets to be somebody else's shift from hell.

So we learn names before the first bottle lands, and we use them all night.
Singles, plenty of them, out early, so nobody working the floor is chasing a
card machine at two in the morning. Please and thank you every time, and I mean
it every time.

My friend still talks about that night. What she took home from it is that these
are beautiful women who can dance, and they are friendly, and they are fun.

And the night gets better for us. Every time.

Okay.

## 1. The Staff Doctrine

Here is my order:

> *A bottle of Dom Pérignon, and a bottle of 1942, and a dozen waters, some Red
> Bulls. Please, thank you so much. Really appreciate you.*

Nothing in that is a technique.

**The floor:**

- Names learned in the first ten minutes, used out loud after that.
- Cash, small bills, early.
- 10% of the night to the host. On a $3K minimum that is $300, in his hand, and
  the number never gets said out loud.
- You order, you never summon. Nobody at my table snaps, waves, or grabs at a
  woman who is working.

Being rude to staff is the loudest thing you can do in a room.

## 2. We Don't Talk Bad About Ourselves in My House

House rule, the way it gets said in my kitchen:

> **"We don't talk bad about ourselves in my house. If you do, you leave."**

No compliment anywhere in that.

When a friend does it in front of you, usually in your mirror while the group is
getting dressed:

1. Do not argue with the content and do not counter-compliment. She has been
   building that case since high school.
2. Close the topic. *Not in my house.* Flat, no speech attached.
3. Hand her the 1942, put Future on.

Anything longer than that is a conversation about how she looks.

Same house, same rule for the apology nobody asked for. Sorry for the seat you
booked. Sorry for asking for water. Two of those in one conversation with nothing
gone wrong, girl, and you are apologizing for occupying space. Now she has to
talk you out of it, which is a job you handed her while feeling humble about it.

## 3. Say It Once

Gratitude is one exchange. Both halves:

> *"Thank you, I had a great time, that was really cool of you."*
>
> *"You're welcome, glad you came out."*

Closed. We don't need to talk about it anymore.

The second round is where it turns. Now the table is discussing what I paid for
and I am sitting there being modest about it. I don't need the attention. I don't
want to talk about me. Just fucking vibe, dude.

**The threshold is one exchange.** If it comes back later, one sentence, warm,
finished. *Stop it, I'm glad you're here.* There is no third round. Which is also
why the $300 never gets announced. Announcing it would be the extra thing.

Same rule, pointed at conflict. The thing I will not do:

> *"I love so-and-so and I think the world of them, blah blah blah, but it really
> hurt me that she said this, but no no no, don't worry, I love her, I think
> she's a great person."*

No. That's fucked up. You already know that I love her. I don't need to do that.

At least not with my inner circle. A loyalty oath in front of a complaint is
insurance, and buying insurance against your own friends is the unkind part.
Inside the circle the preamble is an insult. With somebody you met this year, it
is a map.

**The shape:** the thing, the effect, full stop. *When you said that in front of
the table it hurt me.* Then be quiet and let her answer.

The retraction at the bottom is the worst part, babe. It tells her you didn't
mean the middle.

## 4. Kind Is Not Nice

Nice means agreeing to things. Kind means treating people well while agreeing to
nothing. Nice runs out. Nice says yes at 11 and is furious by 1.

So the no is short, unexplained, and repeated in the same words. *No thank you.*
Same volume, same face, no new information.

Why the same words? Because a reason is something to argue with. *I have a
boyfriend* is now a conversation about a boyfriend. Boring is the objective.

**Two, then the handoff:**

1. *No thank you.* Information.
2. *No thank you.* Identical. A boundary.
3. You stop talking to him and look at security or your host. He is their
   problem now.

You put an arm across, you take the far side of the table, and you only do it
once. Handing it to security is not a favor you are asking, girl.

## 5. Exclusivity Is a Budget

Access is finite. The table seats ten, there are fifty-two Tuesdays in a year,
and you know more people than that. It is arithmetic.

**The table math:** ten seats, eight names you actually want, two left open on
purpose. Somebody always brings somebody. If you do not leave the two, she
arrives anyway and you have no seat to give her.

The gate goes on the invite list. It never goes in your tone. Cold at the door is
what people do when they do not control the door. You can be warm to a woman's
face all night and never text her about the next one.

Everyone at your table is walking around wearing your name. Your friend is rude
to a server, that is yours to handle. Once, quietly, in a sentence. *Hey. Not
with me.* Then you repair it yourself: go back, use her name, tip her separately,
make no speech on anybody's behalf.

**The threshold:** if you would not be willing to remove somebody, do not bring
them. Bringing is vouching. One correction per guest per night, and if it takes
two, she is not a guest anymore.

## 6. How to Ask Someone to Leave

The doctrine, and the whole protocol is inside it:

> **"Okay, I have a problem. I have to take care of it. It's distracting. Let's
> extract the problem and get back to the party. Quick and efficient. No vibe
> kill."**

The goal is not justice. It is the return to the party. You want the room back
before the next song.

Private, not public. Brief, not thorough. Face-saving, not accurate.

**Case one, somebody you are close to.** Ask for a private minute.

> *"Hey, I just don't... it's not you, it's me. We're just not really vibing. We
> haven't seen each other since the first group hang."*

Soft, and slightly untrue on purpose. You are not building a case, you are
providing an exit.

**Case two, somebody another guest brought.** Whoever brought them removes them.
It is faster, and it leaves the awkwardness with the person who created it. You
give her the script, and the script hands him an excuse nobody has checked:

> *"Probably just too high, nervous, but we'd like to lie down for the night
> anyways. So probably be a good idea to go."*

Nobody is accused of anything, which is why he goes fast.

**The tells, in the order they arrive:**

| What you notice | What you do |
|---|---|
| A voice you cannot think through | note it |
| Forced conversation, restarted after it was finished | note it |
| A guest handling your things | note it |
| A woman told how many minutes she is allowed to sleep | decide |
| Everybody upstairs | you are already late |

**The threshold:** *we were basically hiding in the room all night.* If you are
upstairs in a house you pay for, the decision was made hours ago and you are
paying interest on it. The trigger is not somebody being rude. It is the moment
the room reorganizes itself around avoiding one person.

All of that is for annoyed. Frightened is a different category and none of this
applies to it. That one goes to security, the handler, and other people,
immediately.

> **By night type**
> **Club night**: the host and security own the floor. The extraction is theirs.
> **Concert**: nobody is in charge of the man beside you. You move. Two rows back
> is a different night.
> **Festival**: you route around. A different stage, an agreed time.

## 7. The Grace Clause

You are going to be wrong. Loudly, in front of people, about something you were
certain of.

The apology is immediate, short, and has no clause attached. *I was wrong. I'm
sorry.* No *but*. No *I was tired*. Then stop talking. A long apology is another
performance, and now they are hosting you.

Over-tip afterwards. The money is not the apology, it just fixes her shift.

Never litigate it in the room.

**One apology, once.** Repeating it makes her manage your feelings about being
wrong.

### From the Author's Own Files

He came with a friend of mine. Written down, none of it is anything.

He forced conversation, constantly, in a voice I could not think through. He
restarted things that were finished. He picked up a Buddha I have had for ten
years and would not put it down.

Then my friend got sleepy on the couch and he told her, *you get five minutes to
sleep.* And he kept saying it.

That was the moment, and I only found it afterwards, working out when the vibe
actually went. A man telling a tired woman how long she was allowed to close her
eyes, in a house that was not his.

We were basically hiding in the room all night.

So I did the one correct thing. She brought him, so I asked her to handle it. He
was gone quickly.

Then we came downstairs, put the music back up, and got the last hour of the
night.

I learned this one this year. What I had wrong for years was thinking I needed
proof.

## The Princess Protocol

- [ ] Staff names learned early, used out loud after that
- [ ] 10% of the night to the host, cash, and the number never said out loud
- [ ] Nobody talks bad about themselves in your house, including you
- [ ] Thanks: one exchange, no third round
- [ ] Complaints: the thing, the effect, full stop
- [ ] *No thank you*, repeated identically, no new information
- [ ] After the second No, the handoff to security
- [ ] Ten seats, eight names, two left open on purpose
- [ ] One correction per guest per night
- [ ] Whoever brought him walks him out
- [ ] *I was wrong, I'm sorry*, no clause attached

**Never:** rude to anybody working. An accusation where an exit would do. A No
with a reason attached. Thanking somebody into the ground. A complaint that opens
with a loyalty oath. Announcing what you tipped. Correcting a guest in front of
an audience. Removing a guest another guest brought.
