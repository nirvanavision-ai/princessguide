## Four In The Morning

Picture your apartment at four, after a genuinely great night.

You are trying to find a light switch you have used a thousand times. Your
makeup is still on and the pillowcase is about to take it. There is no water
anywhere near the bed. Your phone is at six percent and the charger is behind
something. You are still in the shoes because sitting down to deal with them
felt like a bigger project than staying upright.

Every single one of those was decided at eight o'clock the night before, by you,
while you were busy and cheerful and not thinking about it.

That is the argument of this chapter. Your house is not where you get ready. It
is mission control, and it runs two shifts. The launch, and the return. Most
people staff the first one and leave the second one to a woman who will be in no
condition to do anything about it.

Be nicer to her than that. She had a big night, girl. She is going to be so
tired.

## 1. The Launchpad

One surface. An actual designated one, not the couch, not the end of the bed,
not the floor by the mirror.

On it, before anyone arrives:

- **Chilled Fiji.** Chilled, not frozen, not room temperature. Cold is a gas
  station. Chilled is a decision.
- The bag, loaded, per the arsenal below.
- The backups that are going to the car, in a separate pile so they physically
  cannot end up in the bag.
- One full-length mirror, lit correctly. Overhead light is not lighting.
- The pre-game playlist, which ends when the car arrives and does not get
  renegotiated in the doorway.

The point of the surface is that it makes the missing thing visible. You cannot
see a gap in a pile on a bed. You can see one on a table.

## 2. The Return State

This is the discipline almost nobody runs, and it takes four minutes.

Before you leave, stage the apartment for the woman coming back to it:

- [ ] Water by the bed. Two.
- [ ] Makeup wipes within arm's reach of where you will actually collapse
- [ ] One lamp left on, so nobody is feeling along a wall at four
- [ ] Robe out
- [ ] Charger plugged in and visible, cable already out
- [ ] Something salty in reach of the bed
- [ ] A clear path from the door to the bed with nothing on the floor

Four minutes at eight. It buys back an hour of misery at four and a materially
better Sunday, and it is the cheapest thing in this entire book.

## 3. What Travels

Nothing goes in the bag that you would not hand to a security guard yourself.

That is the whole policy and it is not a moral position, it is arithmetic about
consequences. In Vegas a bag check at the door is standard. What turns up ends
in a property-wide ban that runs a year, and one ownership group is most of the
Strip, so a single confiscated item can end your access to a city. Add the
arrest, which Nevada treats as a real outcome and not a formality.

Then count what that costs everybody else. Your friends are now standing outside
a venue at midnight sorting out your night instead of having theirs. The car is
reassigned. The host has given away your table. The whole apparatus this book
describes gets rerouted to deal with you.

A princess who has to be bailed out has outsourced her logistics to the State of
Nevada.

## 4. The Card

Two names on a physical piece of paper in the bag.

One local, one who knows your medical basics. Written down, on paper, because
the entire scenario the contact exists for is the one where your phone is dead
or gone. You do not know anybody's number anymore. Nobody does.

Alongside it:

- Location sharing on with one named person, with an end time set
- A text on arrival and a text on departure, to that person, as an actual
  protocol and not a nice idea
- The handler from Chapter 3 knows all of it

> **By night type**
> **Festival:** the card matters more here than anywhere, because signal dies
> and everyone assumes it will not. Add a physical meeting point and a time,
> agreed out loud before anyone walks through a gate, plus the RV's location
> written down rather than dropped as a pin.

## 5. The Arsenal

The bag is sized by what it has to hold, which is **phone and sunglasses, plus
a little extra.** That is the ruler. Anything that does not fit that is a
different bag for a different evening.

**What is in it:**

| Tier | Contents |
|---|---|
| Non-negotiable | ID, in the bag and never in the phone case or the shoe. One card. Cash in small bills for tips. Phone. Keys. |
| The two that never leave | **Fenty lip gloss. Listerine strips.** |
| Save-the-night | Blotting papers. Gum. Blister patches. A battery bank with the correct cable. A hair tie, on the inside strap and not your wrist. One safety pin. |
| The wallet | Small, card-sized. Not a wallet with a life of its own. |

Notice what the two non-negotiables have in common. **Gloss and breath strips
are four in the morning items, not ten o'clock items.** Nobody packs for the
end of the night. You do, and that is the entire discipline in two objects
smaller than your hand.

**Banned from the bag:** a full-size anything. Loose change. Three lipsticks
when one is the answer. Receipts. Somebody else's phone, which is how you end up
responsible for it.

### Two rules about the bag itself

**The Weight Rule.** If the strap has left a mark by midnight, the bag is wrong,
and you will find yourself putting it down. Which brings us to the second one.

**The Chair Rule.** The bag does not touch the floor and does not leave your
body. Not on the back of a chair. Not under the table where you can feel it with
a foot. A bag on a chair is a bag you no longer own, and every bag that has ever
gone missing went missing while somebody was having a great time twelve feet
away from it.

## The Princess Protocol

- [ ] One designated launch surface, loaded before anyone arrives
- [ ] Fiji chilled, not frozen
- [ ] Backups staged separately, for the car
- [ ] Return state built before departure: water, wipes, lamp, robe, charger, salt
- [ ] Nothing in the bag you would not hand a guard
- [ ] Emergency card, two names, on paper
- [ ] Location shared with one named person, end time set
- [ ] Arrival and departure texts agreed
- [ ] Bag sized to phone and sunglasses plus extra
- [ ] ID in the bag. Cash in small bills. Fenty gloss. Listerine strips.
- [ ] Battery bank with the correct cable

**Never:** the floor. The back of a chair. A strap that marks you. ID in a phone
case. Anything you would not hand to security. A dead phone with no paper backup.
Somebody else's phone in your bag.
