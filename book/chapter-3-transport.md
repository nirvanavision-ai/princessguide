## The Curb

Here is a scene you have been in. Four adults on a sidewalk at 11:40, phones
out, all four looking at the same app, negotiating. Somebody wants to split into
two cars. Somebody else is saying the price is insane, which it is, because
everybody in the city is trying to leave at once. One of you is in a shoe that
was not built for standing still on concrete. Nobody is having any fun.

That is forty minutes. Not the ten it feels like. Forty, by the time the second
car finds the group and the group finds the door.

Forty minutes is not an inconvenience, girl. It is subtraction. You get a
finite number of nights where the music is good and everyone you like is
already out and nobody has work in the morning, and you just spent a chunk of
one of them doing group math in the cold.

So the car is not a luxury item in this book. It is the thing that stops the
night leaking. Everything in this chapter exists to protect the part you
actually came for, which is the room, and the sound, and eleven people you love
having a night they will bring up for a year.

Transport is where amateurs get found out. It is also where the whole job of
hosting gets done, quietly, before anybody notices there was a job.

## 1. The Brief

Before anything else, this is what a run looks like when it is run properly.
This is what you send your driver:

> *Josh, we need to be there by 11, but I'm going to tell people we need to be
> there at 10:30. Please get there 45 minutes before we need to leave. Get there
> earlier than we need to leave. We're gonna tell people it's time to leave when
> we still have a half hour to an hour left, because we're always late.*
>
> *Please make sure you have 1942 in the cooler. In the back refrigerated
> cooler, not an ice bucket cooler. Ew.*
>
> *Please pick us up at this time. Here is the host's phone number, so he can
> text me when we're ready to go. If you could please get us Gatorades and beef
> jerky, that would be great. We appreciate you so much. Thank you.*

Read it again. Everything in the rest of this chapter is in there.

## 2. The Buffer Doctrine

Look at the clocks in that message. There are four of them and they do not
match, and that is not sloppiness. That is the entire technique.

| Clock | The time | Who is told |
|---|---|---|
| Real arrival | 11:00 | the driver, only |
| Announced arrival | 10:30 | the group |
| Driver on site | 45 min before departure | the driver |
| Announced departure | 30 to 60 min before the real one | the group |

The reason is one line long. *Because we're always late.*

Not *we need to be better about this*. Not a group chat message at 3 p.m. about
respecting each other's time. You are never going to win that fight and it is
boring to keep having. Your friends are late. They have been late for nine
years. They will be late at your funeral.

So you stop treating it as a character flaw and start treating it as a known
quantity, like traffic. You do not argue with traffic. You leave earlier.

That is the difference between a woman who is controlling and a woman who is
competent, and people confuse the two constantly. Controlling is trying to make
eleven adults into punctual people. Competent is building the schedule that
works with the eleven adults you actually have.

**The verdict:** never announce the real time. The real time is between you and
the driver.

## 3. The Vehicle

Book the car on standby for the whole night. Not per leg. Standby.

Per leg means you re-solve the same problem three times, at the three worst
moments to be solving anything: when you're leaving dinner, when you're moving
venues, and at 2 a.m. Standby means it is solved once, at 4 p.m., sober, sitting
down.

What you are actually buying:

- No surge. The 2 a.m. price does not apply to a car that is already yours.
- No cancellation. Nobody drops your ride because a closer fare came in.
- A driver who knows which door. Not the address. The door.
- Nobody's aux cord but ours.

Divide it across the group and it costs less than the argument you would have
had instead. Say that number out loud when you pitch it, because someone always
does the math wrong in their head and decides it's outrageous.

> **By night type**
> **Club night** is what standby was built for. No fixed clock, three possible
> venues, an unknowable end.
> **Concert** is different: someone else set the time, so the car works
> backwards from the set, and the real problem is the exit. Ten thousand people
> leave a venue in the same eight minutes. Have the pickup point be four minutes
> of walking away from where everyone else is standing.
> **Festival**: there is no car. The RV is the car. See below.

## 4. Seating

The front seat is the driver's, the handler's, or nobody's.

This gets read as snobbery and it is actually staging. When the SUV stops, the
rear passenger door opens curbside. Curbside is where the venue is, and the door
team, and the line, and every phone in it. You step out into the room.

The front seat puts you out into traffic, into a blind spot, and into a
half-second of climbing down that has never once looked good on anybody.

The exit is three movements. Bag first. Then both feet to the pavement together.
Then stand. Both feet, not one and then a search for the second. And no hand on
the doorframe. The doorframe is for people who are surprised to be arriving.

Assign the seats in the group thread before anyone leaves the house. Yes, in
writing. The alternative is four adults renegotiating a vehicle on a curb, which
is not a scene, it is a delay, and delays are how a host gives your arrival
window to somebody else.

## 5. The Handler

Somebody in the group is sober tonight and it is a job, not a punishment.

The handler holds the car's number, the host's number, the reservation, and the
real headcount. Rotate the role so it lands on everybody eventually. And give it
one hard authority: **the handler calls the end of the night, and that call is
not relitigated on the sidewalk.**

You want that decided at 6 p.m., by someone who will still be able to think at
2 a.m. Everyone agrees to it easily while sober. That is the whole reason you
agree to it while sober.

## 6. The Cooler and the Console

The playlist is queued before the car moves, and it is sequenced by leg. The
arrival set is not the between-venues set. Two minutes out, bring it down, so
the group steps out talking instead of shouting.

The cooler is where the night gets won, and it is unglamorous. Cold water.
Gatorade. Beef jerky. Gum. A bag of cold towels.

That is electrolytes, salt, and protein, and it is worth more at 1 a.m. than
anything you ordered at 10. The people who end nights early did not end them
because of the night. They ended them because they last ate at four in the
afternoon.

And the tequila lives in the refrigerated cooler in the back. Not an ice bucket.
An ice bucket is a bucket of water with ambitions, and warm 1942 is a personal
failure with a receipt attached.

**The verdict:** if the car is stocked, the night has an extra hour in it. That
hour is the whole point of the car.

## 7. The Trio

Here is the failure this chapter is really preventing.

Three people each hold a third of the plan. The driver knows the pickup point.
The host knows the arrival window. Security knows the exit. And you are standing
on a curb doing the integration yourself, at midnight, in a coat you did not
bring.

So: **security, driver and host all have to be able to reach each other, not
just you.** One thread, made before you leave.

- The driver gets the exact door and a specific pickup pin. Never "out front."
  Out front in Vegas is a quarter mile of identical curb.
- The host gets the arrival window and the true headcount, updated the second it
  changes. Padding a headcount is how you lose a host.
- Security gets the car and the exit plan.
- And the host gets the driver's number, so he can text when you're ready to go.

One sentence in a brief, and you have removed yourself as the bottleneck. That
is the whole trick. **You are not trying to be in the middle of everything. You
are trying to not be needed.**

### From the Author's Own Files

The cabin is a controlled environment and this is why.

I was in full glam, in a moving vehicle, on the way to a Kanye show. The
boyfriend at the time reached over to help with something on my face.

He poked me in the eye.

He did not apologize. He was a nuisance for the rest of the night, and then he
did the thing that actually ended it, which was to spend an evening at a Kanye
concert trying to make the evening about himself. But that comes later, in
another chapter, because that is a different category of problem.

The car part is simple and it is a rule now. **Nobody touches your face in a
moving vehicle.** Not to help. Especially not to help. Help gets offered, and
then it gets performed at a red light, or in a parking structure, or not at all.

There is no repair you can do at 40 miles an hour that is better than the repair
you can do standing still, and the number of things that can go wrong with a
lash, a liner or an eye in a car that is changing lanes is not a number worth
discovering the way I discovered it.

> **By night type**
> **Concert:** the car is on a deadline it did not set. Reverse-schedule from
> the set time, not from dinner, and take the support act seriously as a hard
> marker.
> **Festival:** the RV replaces the car and the home base at once. Somewhere to
> sit, somewhere to change, somewhere cold, and a fixed physical rendezvous that
> works when nobody has signal. Agree the meeting point and the times out loud
> before anyone walks through a gate.

## The Princess Protocol

- [ ] Car booked on standby for the whole night, not per leg
- [ ] Brief sent to the driver: real time, announced time, pickup pin, cooler
- [ ] Announced arrival 30 min before real arrival
- [ ] Driver on site 45 min before departure
- [ ] Departure announced 30 to 60 min before the real one
- [ ] Seats assigned in the thread before anyone leaves the house
- [ ] Handler named, sober, holding every number, with authority to call the end
- [ ] Playlist queued and sequenced by leg, volume down two minutes out
- [ ] Cooler: water, Gatorade, beef jerky, gum, cold towels
- [ ] 1942 in the refrigerated cooler
- [ ] One thread with driver, host and security in it, made before you leave
- [ ] Host has the driver's number

**Never:** the front seat. A pickup point described as "out front." A padded
headcount. Renegotiating seats on a curb. Anybody's hands near your face while
the car is moving. Warm tequila.
