## Nine O'Clock

There is a girl getting ready right now, at nine, for a night that starts at
eleven. She is doing her lashes with wet nails. She has not eaten since two. The
dress is one she has never sat down in. The shoes are new, and she is about to
find out something about them in a parking structure.

She will have a fine night. Fine is available to everybody.

The nights people talk about for a year are built on a different schedule, and
the schedule started yesterday morning. Not because getting ready is a spiritual
practice. Because everything that has to *set* needs a day to set in, and
everything that can go wrong is cheaper to fix on a Tuesday afternoon than at
10:40 with a car downstairs.

This is the least glamorous chapter in the book and it decides most of the
outcome. Every hour you spend on it buys back two later: the hour you do not
lose to a shoe, the hour you do not lose to a fitting room, the hour you do not
lose lying on a bathroom floor at one because you skipped lunch and drank
champagne on it.

You are not getting ready, girl. You are removing the reasons the night could
end early.

## 2. Water Is Not a Vibe

Most nights that end at one do not end because of the night. They end because
somebody was already down two litres when the first drink landed.

Hydration starts a full day out and it is water plus electrolytes, because
plain water at that volume is going straight through you. Then it continues
inside the venue, which is why there are a dozen waters on the table in Chapter
4 and Gatorade in the cooler in Chapter 3. The pattern is the same every time:
build it into the order so nobody has to remember it at two in the morning.

**On IV drips.** If you use them, book the day before. A mobile provider with a
same-day slot at six on a Saturday is a fantasy, and the good ones are booked
out. It is a medical service, it goes through a licensed provider, and anybody
who does not ask what you take before they put a line in you is not one. That
is the whole of my opinion on it.

## 3. The Fitting, Not the Outfit

Order three sizes. Keep one. Send two back inside the window, because a return
window is a deadline like any other and a missed one is a hundred dollars you
set on fire for no reason.

Then test the one you kept, in the room, before the night exists:

- **Sit down in it.** All the way down. On a low seat.
- **Both arms straight up.** That is the dancing test and the photo test at once.
- **Photograph it under flash.** Club lighting is mostly flash and fabric lies
  in daylight. You want to find out about the lining now.
- **Walk in it.** Not to the mirror. Around.

## 4. Yellow Lenses

Buy the yellow-tinted sunglasses.

Dark lenses stop working the moment the sun goes down, which is the only time
you are actually out. So you either take them off, or you spend the night
looking like a woman who does not know she is indoors. A yellow tint solves it.
It lifts a dim room, it warms everything, and it reads as a choice rather than a
recovery.

Then it works again in the morning, over breakfast, for entirely different
reasons. One accessory, both ends of the night, and the only one you own that is
doing a job at 1 a.m. and at 11 a.m.

## 5. The Shoe

Here is the doctrine, and it is not about height.

**Jimmy Choo, and specifically the platform-to-heel ratio.** A thicker heel than
a stiletto with a platform under the front of the foot changes the angle your
ankle is held at for six hours. Surface area does the rest: a platform survives
a sticky floor, a curb, a grate, and a sprint when somebody says they are
closing the list.

And the part nobody says out loud: **your feet will not hurt, and your friends
will not complain to you that their feet hurt.** You are choosing your own shoes
partly so you are not managing eleven other pairs at one in the morning.

Two more rules, both hers:

- **Nude, not strapped**, when you want the leg long. Straps chop it.
- The **YSL Tribute sandal** is the most comfortable all-around shoe you can put
  on, and it is the answer on any night you are not sure about.

### The Two-Lap Test

T-minus 90. Shoes on, fully dressed, and you walk your apartment. Hallway,
kitchen, back through the living room, around the coffee table. That is one.
Then you do it again.

If the second lap hurts, the shoe does not come.

It is arithmetic. A club night is thousands of steps, a meaningful number of
them on a floor with the traction of a fresh oyster, and your apartment is flat,
carpeted and cooler than anywhere you are going. A shoe losing in the easiest
conditions it will ever face is not staging a comeback in a coat check line.

There is no third lap. The third lap is denial with cardio.

**And the rule that governs all of it:** never take your heels off. We are not
friends. Taking your shoes off at a table is an intimacy you have not earned
with this room, which is exactly why the shoe has to be survivable in the first
place. Backups live in the car. Not the bag. The car.

## 6. Where You Go, and Why

Before the codes, the thing that decides them.

You are going for the music. Not the room's reputation, not the photograph, not
who else is going. That is the whole selection criterion and almost nobody uses
it, which is why so many people spend a lot of money to stand somewhere famous
and quiet.

Which produces a rule most people get backwards: **the biggest show is not the
best night.** The marquee one is the one you would watch again. A smaller one is
the one you actually enjoyed. Both are worth going to and they are not the same
category, so stop being disappointed when the enormous show is merely
impressive.

Rooms are judged on sound. **M2 in Miami. Academy in Los Angeles.** Two rooms is
a shorter list than you were expecting and that is the point: a sound system
does not change hands, and a club does. Which is also why this book will not
hand you a list of venues. Clubs turn over. The behaviour does not.

**Los Angeles.** Overrated for nightlife, and I say that as somebody who is
there constantly. The door is a social credit check and everybody is performing
being unbothered, so the local dialect is to be unbothered back. Arrive early,
11:15 rather than 12:30. Small groups. The door reads the group, never the girl.
Your host's name is the password and you say it once, calmly, and then you stop
talking.

**Las Vegas.** The most overrated city in America for this. Security is real
security. Wands, bag checks, and a property-wide ban that runs a year, which
matters more than any fine because one ownership group is most of the Strip.
Distances are a lie. That walk is fifteen minutes indoors, on carpet, which is
where the shoe doctrine stops being an opinion.

**Miami.** Sparkle is the dress code and it is enforced. Nothing starts before
one and pretending otherwise marks you as visiting. Heat and humidity are a
styling constraint before they are a comfort one: setting spray, hair that
survives, nothing that shows a drop.

I am in Miami, bitch.

> **By night type**
> **Concert:** the set time is the only clock that matters and everything above
> reverse-schedules from it. Check the bag policy before you leave, because many
> arenas run size limits and a clear-bag rule, and finding that out at the wand
> is how you end up with a bag in a bin.
> **Festival:** the whole timeline compresses into a packing problem, and the
> footwear doctrine inverts completely. Boots. Never heels. Miles of walking,
> sun for eight hours, cold after dark.

## 8. Know What You Are Holding

Written cold, because the alternative is a lecture and nobody has ever changed
anything because of one.

Never leave a drink. Never take an open one from a stranger. Hand over the glass
when you are moving through a room. Know what is in what you are handed, and
know the law in the state you are standing in, because at a Vegas door a search
is routine and what it turns up ends in a ban, an arrest, or both. Either way
somebody else is now running your night, and the entire premise of this book is
that you run it.

Eat. Hydrate on the schedule. The objective is to leave on your own feet, in
your own shoes, with your own phone.

from a stranger. A bag you have not checked against the venue's policy. A dress
you have never sat down in.
