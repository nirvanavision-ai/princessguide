## The Line

Here is my whole position on doors, and it is going to sound like a flex before
it sounds like logistics.

I buy a table. So I do not have to be out there doing any of that.

Doing any of that means standing in a line in a shoe, being assessed by a man
with a clipboard who is running ratios in his head, texting a promoter you met
once, and arriving inside forty minutes later already annoyed and already cold.
All of which is a service you can simply purchase, and the price of it is
knowable in advance.

Which reframes what a table actually is. It is not a trophy you get for being
important enough to be let in. It is the mechanism by which you are not standing
outside. You are paying a minimum in exchange for time, position, and a place to
put a bag, and the sooner you think of it as a commercial arrangement wearing a
party hat, the sooner you stop overpaying for it.

Read the contract, girl. Everybody else in that room is improvising.

## 1. Recon

Before you go anywhere, look up the menu. The whole thing.

- **The minimum is a spend floor, not an entry fee.** You are not buying a
  ticket, you are agreeing to spend at least that much on things you will then
  own. Different arithmetic entirely.
- **The service charge is not the tip.** Twenty percent auto-added does not
  reach your server the way you think it does, and it does not touch the host at
  all. Assume you are tipping on top and price the night accordingly.
- **Read the table map.** Dance-floor adjacency, distance to the booth, and the
  one that looks incredible in photographs and sits next to a service corridor.
- **Read reviews for the operational tells only.** Ignore anybody describing the
  vibe. Count how many complaints mention the check.

## 2. The Host

The number is **ten percent of the night.** On a three-thousand-dollar minimum,
that is three hundred dollars, in his hand.

Say what it buys, because people get vague about this and then feel robbed.
It is the host taking you to your table. It is skipping the line and getting in
quickly. That is the transaction, and it is a good one, and it is worth
budgeting as a line item rather than producing as a surprise.

But the tip is not the relationship. One host cultivated over a year beats
cold-tipping five, every time, because the fifth time you text him he already
knows the headcount you actually run and whether you close your check. So:

- Text him on a Tuesday, not only at 11:50 on a Saturday
- Give him the true headcount, always. Padding it once is how you stop being
  somebody he answers
- Cash, discreetly
- **Never announce the number.** Announcing it would be the extra thing, and
  Chapter 5 explains why the extra thing is the rude one

## 3. The Photographer

Somebody in the group is on photos and you name them out loud, the way you name
the handler.

Not the person with the best camera. The person who **remembers**, and who makes
sure everybody gets one. Including the friend who never asks. Including the girl
who came alone and is having a good time and will have nothing to show for it.

This is a real job and it is invisible when it is done well. A year from now
nobody remembers the bottle. Somebody is going to want a picture with the friend
she came for, and either that photo exists or it does not, and the difference is
whether one person was quietly paying attention at midnight.

## 4. The Sign

You get a custom sign with the bottle. Custom is the operative word.

**The one that worked: DANCE BITCHES.** At Academy. And everybody danced, a lot,
all night. It is an instruction. It points at the room rather than at a person,
it gives fifty strangers something to do, and it made the night better for
people who were not even at the table.

**The one that did not.** A different city, a different room, and what arrived
was a stock sign off a pile: FUCK MY BABY MAMA.

I am a woman. I cannot possibly have a baby mama.

So think about what that actually tells you. Nobody in the building looked up.
Nobody asked. There is a stack of pre-made signs by the service bar and somebody
grabbed the top one and walked it out to a table of women. It is not a taste
failure. It is a service failure, and it was not the only one that night: the
1942 came out warm, which is disgusting, the singles did not move, and the whole
thing was boring.

We left.

**The rule.** A sign is an invitation or it is a liability. If it points at the
room, it works. If it points at a person, or if it was clearly written for
somebody who is not you, the people running that room are not paying attention,
and everything else about your night is going to be run by those same people.

**The Warm Bottle** is the fastest read there is. A room that sends out a
five-hundred-dollar bottle at room temperature has told you, in about four
seconds and before you have spent anything else, exactly how much attention the
rest of the evening is going to get. Believe it the first time.

## 5. The Table Economy

The single highest-yield thing in this chapter takes ninety seconds and happens
before the first bottle lands.

**Settle the split in writing, in the group thread, while everyone is sober.**
Who is on the minimum. Who is a guest. What happens if the spend goes over. Then
screenshot it.

Nobody has ever had this conversation successfully at 2 a.m. It is the single
most reliable way to lose a friend over an amount of money neither of you will
remember in a year.

The rest of it:

- **You pour, or somebody you named pours.** A table where anybody pours runs
  out at one.
- **Guests are guests.** A guest who invites six more people is not a guest, she
  is a cost centre, and that is a conversation for Chapter 5.
- **Close the check before the lights come up.** Not after. After is when the
  disputes happen and when nobody can find the host.

> **By night type**
> **Concert:** most of this does not apply. The spend is front-loaded into the
> ticket, there is nothing to negotiate on the night, and the only real decision
> is a box versus the floor. The photographer still matters. So does the split,
> if anybody bought in a block.
> **Club night:** everything above, exactly as written.

## 6. The Romance Variable

A partner at the table is not a problem. A partner who cannot let the night be
about the night is a structural one, and you will know the difference within
about twenty minutes.

The pre-brief, before you leave the house: what the night is, who it is for, and
when it ends. A partner who arrives having agreed to all three is a guest. A
partner who arrives to supervise has turned a girls' night into a hostage
situation with better lighting.

Then there is the general category of men who need the room to notice them. The
one who has to go and talk to the DJ. The friend's boyfriend ordering on
somebody else's tab. The guest who turns the check into a competition.

De-escalation is an operational skill and it has three rules. Never argue in the
room. Hand it to the host. Relocate the person rather than the party.

You are the general manager of this table. General managers do not have scenes.
They make staffing decisions.

### From the Author's Own Files

The night of a Kanye concert, in the car, in full glam.

The boyfriend at the time reached over to help with something on my face and
poked me in the eye. He did not apologise. Then he spent the evening trying to
move the attention off Kanye and onto himself, which he kept at all night, and
which was the actual problem.

Read the order of that. The eye was an accident. Not apologising was rude.
Making himself the event, at a show I had been waiting for, at a show eleven
other people had been waiting for, is the one that ended it. I dumped him after.

He called it emotional execution.

I called it a Sunday night.

### And the best one

Here is the other half of the same idea, and it is the more useful half.

The best night I have had recently was not a big one. It was around my birthday
and the reason it was the best is that **people were not making it about my
birthday.** Low-pressure dressing. Cargo jeans and a crop top. Nobody fawning
over anybody. The music was great, everything flowed, the sign said dance
bitches, and everybody danced a lot.

That is the whole test, and it applies to boyfriends and to birthday girls
equally. **A night organised around a person is worse than a night organised
around the music.** Which means the standard you are holding him to is one you
have to hold yourself to first, on the one night of the year you would be
entitled not to.

## The Princess Protocol

- [ ] Menu, minimum, service charge and table map read before leaving
- [ ] Host texted in advance, true headcount given
- [ ] Ten percent of the night, cash, discreet, never announced
- [ ] Photographer named out loud
- [ ] Split settled in writing in the thread before the first bottle
- [ ] Sign is custom and points at the room
- [ ] Bottle temperature checked on arrival
- [ ] Someone named to pour
- [ ] Check closed before the lights
- [ ] Partner pre-briefed on what the night is and when it ends

**Never:** a padded headcount. Announcing the number. A sign with a person in
it. Arguing in the room. Splitting a tab at two in the morning. A guest who
brings guests. A warm bottle you decide to be fine about.
