### What generosity looks like to you

> *"Just respect and kindness, common courtesy. But not being extra."*
>
> *"I really don't like when people over-thank me… when they keep going on about
> it, and they keep going on. I don't need the attention. I don't want — just
> fucking vibe, dude. I don't want to talk about me. Like, be nice, like 'thank
> you, I had a great time, that was really cool of you.' 'You're welcome, glad
> you came out.' No, we don't need to talk about it anymore."*
>
> *"Also helping friends when they need help."*

> **This is the most original etiquette rule in the entire book and it belongs in
> Chapter 5.** Every book in this genre teaches gratitude. None of them teach
> **how to stop.** Your rule: thanks is one exchange, warmly given, warmly
> received, closed. Continuing to thank someone turns their generosity into a
> conversation about them, and that's the actual rudeness. *"I don't want to talk
> about me. Just fucking vibe, dude."*
>
> It also explains the $300: you tip 10% and then never mention it, because
> mentioning it would be the extra thing.

## Correction to the manuscript

The current Chapter 4 spec says **"$300 upfront host tipping."** That's wrong as
a flat figure — your rule is **10% of the night**, and $300 is what 10% looks
like on a $3K minimum. Scaling beats a fixed number: it travels to any venue and
any spend, and it's a rule a reader can actually apply. Updated in the spec.
