# The Voice Knowledge Base

This folder is the answer to "how do I make it sound like *me* and not like a
robot with a subscription to a fashion newsletter."

Here's the honest mechanic. Generic writing happens when a writer knows the
*category* but not the *instance*. "A trendy nightclub" is category. "Delilah on
a Tuesday, because Saturday is for people who don't know better" is instance.
Category is what anyone can produce. Instance is what only you have.

So this knowledge base has one job: get your instances out of your head and onto
disk, where the skill can quote them instead of inventing around them.

## The five files, ranked by how much they matter

| File | What it captures | Payoff |
|------|------------------|--------|
| [`samples.md`](samples.md) | Raw things you've actually written | ★★★★★ |
| [`world.md`](world.md) | Your venues, brands, drinks, songs, cities | ★★★★★ |
| [`verdicts.md`](verdicts.md) | Your opinions, ethics, hills to die on | ★★★★ |
| [`lexicon.md`](lexicon.md) | Your words, phrases, sentence habits | ★★★ |
| [`quotes.md`](quotes.md) | Lines you write yourself, for epigraphs and pull quotes | ★★★ |

**If you only do one thing, do `samples.md`.** Twenty of your real texts,
captions, or voice-note transcripts teach me more than a thousand words of you
describing your own style. People are bad at describing their own voice and
excellent at demonstrating it. That's not a knock — it's true of everyone. Your
rhythm is invisible to you because you're standing inside it.

## The 20-minute fast path

Nobody fills out a workbook cover to cover. Do this instead:

1. **Five minutes.** Open your phone. Copy twenty messages you've sent that made
   someone laugh, or that you'd stand behind in writing. Paste into `samples.md`.
   Don't clean them up. Typos are data.
2. **Ten minutes.** Open `world.md` and fill in only the lines you can answer
   instantly, without thinking. The ones that make you pause, skip. Anything you
   have to *construct* isn't your voice anyway.
3. **Five minutes.** Open `verdicts.md` and write down three things about going
   out that you're right about and other people are wrong about. Be unfair.
   Unfair is where voice lives.

That's enough to start. The rest fills in as we draft — when a chapter needs
something I don't have, I'll ask you for that one thing rather than sending you
back to a form.

## How this gets used

`/party-princess` reads this folder before every chapter and:

- **Quotes your samples for rhythm** — sentence length, how you open, whether
  you use fragments, where you land a joke.
- **Pulls real nouns from `world.md`** instead of generic ones. This alone kills
  most of the AI smell.
- **Argues your positions from `verdicts.md`**, not the median internet opinion.
- **Uses your words from `lexicon.md`** and avoids the ones you'd never say.
- **Drops your lines from `quotes.md`** into the epigraph and pull-quote slots.

## Two rules

**Nothing in here gets rewritten.** These files are yours. The skill reads them
and never edits them. If something is half-finished, misspelled, or a fragment,
leave it — that's more useful to me than a polished version.

**Nothing gets invented to fill a gap.** If a slot is empty, the chapter gets a
placeholder block asking you for it. An empty slot is honest. A fabricated
memory is the one thing that would sink this book.
